- **What worked:**
  - one probe with different signed values per mask decoded the three AI masks in a single render (Subject = whole rock, Sky includes clouds, Vegetation unused).
  - the hero separated with parametric intersects (Subject ∩ gradient ∩ Luminance), including a chained double intersect.
  - the sky glow was measured from the original (the sky's own gradient), not guessed; the polariser band (x 50–60%, L\* 5) is visible in a stretched-tone view.
  - the AI Sky mask was replaced by gradient ∩ Luminance − Subject, which cut the open-sky speckle at 1:1 from 1.43 to 0.62.
- **What went wrong / lessons:**
  - **My sky lift made the glow worse:** Exposure is multiplicative, so the brighter edges gained +5.5 and the dark centre +2.6…4.3; the edge-to-interior gap stayed at about 8 while the whole sky got brighter. The glow was already in the file (left edge 17 vs interior 10, dark band at 5).
  - **Speckle at the cloud edge came from the AI Sky mask's coarse edge** (`FullMaskSize 2879×1920`, stops about 30 px short of the cloud) combined with Luminance fades. I first blamed 'Cloud calm' from a patchiness metric; the user found it was the Sky lift. The effect-versus-all-off speckle metric agreed with the user.
  - **A cancelled Denoise visit left the three AI-Sky corrections not rendering** (effect exactly 0.0000 in 2000 px and 1:1) until the user refreshed the mask in the dialog.
  - **Hero burn overshoot:** Exp −0.14 / Highlights −0.30 / Whites −0.25 took the lit faces down −17 L\* (forecast −8…−12); I backed it off to −0.10 / −0.22 / −0.18 (−12).
  - **Units:** the user's "−0.15" was dialog units (−0.0375 stored); I wrote −0.15 stored (dialog −0.60) from my own proposal.
  - **Wrong caution:** I warned that a brush could be dropped on the object mask 'left ridge'; its Paint is the object-selection gesture, not a brush table.
  - **Wrong zone:** I said the vignette subtract was over the brightest sky; I had read the far-right edge strip, not the user's footprint.
  - **Focal length:** the user said 47 mm; EXIF says 24 mm. I built an argument on 47 mm (the polariser band would cross the frame as a ~40° arc); at 24 mm the field is about 74° wide, which makes a polariser gradient even more likely.
- **Not checked:** noise at 100% in the lifted left ridge and sky (no Denoise; the user looked and accepted); the saved TIFF re-read from disk; print; the front boulder's lit face (5% of that zone with a channel above 0.97).
